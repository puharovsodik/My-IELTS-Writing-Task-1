Why Tables Are Different — Know Your Diagram!

Before you write a single word, understand what tables demand

Tables are comparison-heavy diagrams. Unlike line graphs (which show trends over time) or pie charts (which show proportions), tables present multiple categories across multiple variables simultaneously. Your job is to identify the most striking comparisons, group similar data, and avoid describing every single cell — that will kill your Task Achievement score.

▼ SAMPLE TABLE (used throughout this guide)
Country GDP per capita ($) Life Expectancy Literacy Rate (%) Internet Access (%)
Norway 82,500 82.5 100 98
Brazil 8,920 75.9 93 70
India 2,100 69.7 74 43
Ethiopia 925 66.2 38 15

🔎 Yellow = highest values | Red = lowest values — these are your KEY FEATURES to mention!

The first 2 sentences that set your Band score ceiling

PART A — Paraphrase the Question (Sentence 1)

Never copy the question. Paraphrase using synonyms for "shows," "compares," and the topic noun. Tables almost always compare things across categories — that verb is your friend.

Band 6 — Safe
The table provides information about four development indicators across four selected countries.
Band 6 — Safe
The table presents data on key economic and social metrics for Norway, Brazil, India, and Ethiopia.
Band 8+ — Sophisticated
The table compares four countries across a range of economic and social development indicators, namely GDP per capita, life expectancy, literacy rate, and internet access.
Band 8+ — Sophisticated
Drawn from a cross-national dataset, the table illustrates striking disparities in living standards and connectivity among four nations at differing stages of development.

PART B — The Overview Statement (Sentence 2) — Your Most Important Sentence!

The overview is where Band 6 students lose points and Band 8 students gain them. For tables, your overview must state the single clearest overall pattern — usually which category/country ranks highest/lowest, or that there is a wide range of values. Never include specific numbers here.

Band 6 — Safe
Overall, Norway ranks highest in all four categories, while Ethiopia records the lowest figures across most indicators.
Band 8+ — Sophisticated
Overall, a clear north-south divide is evident, with wealthier nations consistently outperforming lower-income countries across every metric presented, most dramatically in terms of internet access.
Band 8+ — Sophisticated
In general, what stands out most is the considerable variation between nations, with Norway leading in all indicators and Ethiopia lagging considerably behind the other three countries.
🏆 Secret Weapon for Table Overviews: Look for the "biggest gap" between the highest and lowest value in any one column. Mentioning this gap (without the numbers) instantly shows you can interpret data, not just describe it — and that's Band 8 thinking!

🚫 Don't Say...

The table shows a lot of information about countries. → Too vague. What kind of information? Name the variables.
Overall, Norway has $82,500 GDP per capita. → NEVER include specific numbers in the overview!
In conclusion, the table is very interesting. → Don't use "In conclusion" — use "Overall" or "In general."

Key Description Vocabulary Patterns

Comparison, ranking, and feature language for tables

Tables require three types of language: ranking language (highest/lowest), comparison language (more than/less than), and gap language (much higher than, approximately double). Master all three.

🥇 Ranking Language — Describing Extremes

Band 6
Norway had the highest GDP per capita, at $82,500.
Band 6
Ethiopia recorded the lowest literacy rate of all four countries, at just 38%.
Band 8+
Ranking highest across all indicators, Norway stood out markedly from its counterparts, with a GDP per capita of $82,500 — nearly 90 times greater than Ethiopia's figure of $925.
Band 8+
Internet access showed the most pronounced disparity, ranging from a mere 15% in Ethiopia to 98% in Norway.

📊 Comparison Language — Contrasting Two Items

Band 6
Brazil had a higher life expectancy than India, at 75.9 years compared to 69.7 years.
Band 8+
While Brazil and India occupied a middle ground in most indicators, Brazil outperformed India in life expectancy by over six years and in internet access by a substantial margin of 27 percentage points.
▼ SENTENCE ANATOMY — Band 8 Comparison Structure
[While/Although] + [Subject A] + [verb phrase], [Subject B] + [outperformed/exceeded/surpassed] + [Subject A] + in [category] + by [gap]

The "by [gap]" ending shows the examiner you can quantify differences — instant Band 7+ signal

🚫 Don't Say...

Norway is more high than India. → Norway ranked considerably higher than India / Norway's figures were substantially higher than India's.
The difference between Norway and Ethiopia is very big. → Norway substantially outpaced Ethiopia in every category. / A wide gulf separated Norway and Ethiopia across all metrics.
India's internet access is 43% and Ethiopia's is 15%. → India's internet access (43%) was nearly three times that of Ethiopia (15%).

Comparison & Grouping Structures

How to organize your body paragraphs like an expert

This is where most students go wrong: they describe the table row by row (country by country) or column by column — which feels mechanical and scores low. The secret is to group your data meaningfully. For tables, use one of these two approaches:

📐 Two Expert Grouping Strategies:
Strategy A (Most Common): Group by performance tier — e.g., high-performing countries vs. low-performing ones.
Strategy B: Group by indicator category — e.g., economic indicators first (GDP), then social (life expectancy, literacy), then technological (internet).

Grouping Sentence Starters (Tier-Based)

Band 6
In terms of economic indicators, Norway and Brazil performed significantly better than India and Ethiopia.
Band 6
Looking at the two highest-ranked countries, both Norway and Brazil showed relatively strong figures across most categories.
Band 8+
Grouping the countries into two tiers, Norway and Brazil consistently outperformed the remaining two, though even within this upper group, Norway stood in a category of its own, ranking first in all four indicators.
Band 8+
Across all four categories, a clear pattern of economic stratification emerges: higher-income nations achieve markedly superior outcomes in social and technological development alike.

Similarity Structures — Showing What's Comparable

Examiners reward students who can identify not just differences but also similarities. Use these to show sophisticated analysis:

Band 6
Both India and Brazil had similar literacy rates, at 74% and 93% respectively.
Band 8+
A notable parallel can be observed between India and Brazil regarding life expectancy: both countries fell within a comparable range of 69–76 years, in contrast to the extremes represented by Norway (82.5) and Ethiopia (66.2).
Band 8+
Interestingly, all four nations shared relatively high literacy rates — with the exception of Ethiopia, which lagged far behind at 38% — suggesting that basic education has reached a wider population than digital or economic development.
▼ PARAGRAPH STRUCTURE MAP — Body Paragraph 1 (Tier-Based)
Sentence 1: Introduce the tier/grouping ("Turning to the higher-performing countries...")
↓
Sentence 2: State the standout value ("Norway ranked first in all indicators, most notably...")
↓
Sentence 3: Contrast with next country in same group ("Brazil, while lower, also...")
↓
Sentence 4 (optional): Quantify the gap or add a notable similarity

This 3–4 sentence paragraph structure earns Band 7+ for Task Achievement & Coherence

🚫 Don't Say...

Norway had 82,500. Brazil had 8,920. India had 2,100. Ethiopia had 925. → This "list" style is Band 4–5. Group and compare instead!
In the first column, Norway is 82,500 and Brazil is 8,920. → Never describe "columns" — describe categories by name. "In terms of GDP per capita..."

Time, Sequence & Transitional Expressions

Linking ideas and organizing your response with Band 7+ cohesion

Unlike line graphs, tables usually show static data at one point in time. So most tense usage should be simple past (if a year is given) or simple present (if no year or if it's generally true). The "time" expressions for tables are really organizational transitions — you're moving between categories and groups, not through time.

⏱ Quick Tense Rule for Tables:
— Table with a specific year (e.g. "in 2020") → Use simple past: "Norway had the highest GDP..."
— Table with no specific year → Use simple present: "Norway has the highest GDP..." or "Norway ranks first..."
— Never mix tenses without reason!

Organizational Transitions (Moving Between Data Groups)

Band 6
Regarding internet access, Norway led with 98%, while Ethiopia had only 15%.
Band 6
With respect to life expectancy, all four countries fell within a relatively narrow range of 66 to 83 years.
Band 8+
Shifting focus to social indicators, the picture becomes more nuanced: literacy rates were consistently high across the first three countries, ranging between 74% and 100%, though Ethiopia proved a notable outlier at 38%.
Band 8+
Turning to the lower end of the spectrum, India and Ethiopia presented considerably weaker figures, particularly in economic and digital development indicators.

Sequencing Within a Paragraph (Listing Multiple Data Points)

When you need to mention several values from the same category, use sequencing phrases to avoid repetition:

In descending order, internet access figures stood at 98% (Norway), 70% (Brazil), 43% (India), and 15% (Ethiopia), reflecting each country's level of economic development.
At the other extreme, Ethiopia recorded the lowest values in three of the four categories, with particularly poor performance in literacy and internet connectivity.

Transition Phrase Bank

Purpose Expression
Moving to next categoryTurning to... / Shifting focus to... / Regarding...
Adding contrastIn contrast... / By comparison... / On the other hand...
Adding similaritySimilarly... / Likewise... / In the same vein...
Signalling exceptionsWith the exception of... / Ethiopia aside... / Notably...
Describing extremesAt the top end... / At the other extreme... / At the lower end of the spectrum...
Summarizing a groupAcross all categories... / In all metrics... / Taken together...

🚫 Don't Say...

First... Second... Third... Fourth... (for each country) → These ordinal sequences sound like a list, not an analysis. Use category transitions instead.
In 2020, Norway will have the highest GDP. → Never use future tense when describing past or present table data.
Also, moreover, furthermore (used 5+ times) → Vary your transitions! Overusing "also" signals Band 5–6 coherence.

Data Integration Techniques

Where and how to place specific figures for maximum impact

This is your final superpower. Raw numbers sitting alone in a sentence are worth nothing to the examiner. Numbers must be framed, contextualized, and compared. Learn these four techniques and your Task Achievement score will jump.

Technique 1 — The Bracket Method (Safest, Most Efficient)

State the comparative claim first, then bracket the specific numbers as supporting evidence. This keeps your writing analytical rather than descriptive.

Band 6 — Numbers Before Comparison
Norway had $82,500 GDP per capita. This was higher than Brazil's $8,920. ❌ (Two separate sentences, no comparison word)
Band 8+ — Comparison First, Numbers in Brackets
Norway substantially outpaced all three other nations in terms of GDP per capita, recording a figure of $82,500 — compared to $8,920 for Brazil, $2,100 for India, and a mere $925 for Ethiopia.

Technique 2 — The Multiplier (Shows Relative Scale — Very Impressive)

Instead of just listing two numbers, calculate the ratio. This shows the examiner you can interpret data, not just copy it.

Norway's GDP per capita was approximately nine times greater than Brazil's and nearly 90 times that of Ethiopia, highlighting the profound wealth gap between the two extremes.
Internet access in Norway was more than six times that recorded in Ethiopia (98% versus 15%), representing one of the most striking disparities in the table.

Technique 3 — The Qualifying Adverb (Adds Nuance)

Wrap your numbers in precise adverbs to add precision without adding length:

AdverbUse When...Example
just / only / a mereValue is surprisingly low"a mere 15% internet access"
approximately / roughly / aroundYou're rounding"roughly 75 years"
marginally / slightlySmall difference"marginally higher than Brazil"
considerably / substantially / markedlyLarge difference"considerably outperforming India"
nearly / almost / just underClose to a round number"nearly 100% literacy"

Technique 4 — The Exception Clause (Shows Analysis)

Band 8+ writers notice when one data point breaks a pattern. The exception clause lets you state a general trend AND acknowledge the outlier elegantly:

Band 8+
With the exception of Ethiopia, literacy rates were relatively high across all nations, ranging from 74% in India to a perfect 100% in Norway — suggesting that basic education has made broader gains than economic development in these countries.
Band 8+
Although Brazil and India differed considerably in wealth, their life expectancy figures were relatively comparable — 75.9 and 69.7 years respectively — an anomaly worth noting given the stark economic contrast between the two nations.
🏆 Secret Weapon for Data Integration: Always ask "So what?" after you mention a number. Add a brief interpretive phrase — "suggesting that...", "highlighting...", "reflecting..." — and you instantly sound like a Band 8 candidate who is analyzing, not just reporting.

🚫 Don't Say...

The data is 82,500 for Norway and 925 for Ethiopia. → Never use "the data is [number]." Say: "Norway recorded a GDP of $82,500, dwarfing Ethiopia's figure of $925."
Norway's percentage is 98%. Ethiopia's percentage is 15%. → Avoid "percentage is." Use: "internet access stood at / reached / was recorded at"
Including every single data point from the table. → NEVER describe all data — select the most significant figures only. This is Task Achievement!

Complete Model Response — Band 8 Example

See every technique working together in a full response

The table compares four nations — Norway, Brazil, India, and Ethiopia — across four key development indicators: GDP per capita, life expectancy, literacy rate, and internet access.

Overall, a clear hierarchy is evident, with Norway consistently ranking first across all four categories, while Ethiopia lagged considerably behind the other nations in most respects.

In terms of economic performance, Norway's GDP per capita of $82,500 represented approximately 90 times that of Ethiopia ($925), highlighting the profound wealth gap between the two extremes. Brazil and India occupied a middle ground at $8,920 and $2,100 respectively.

Shifting focus to social indicators, life expectancy figures fell within a relatively narrow range of 66 to 83 years across all countries, suggesting that basic healthcare has made broader gains than economic development. With the exception of Ethiopia (38%), literacy rates were high, ranging from 74% in India to 100% in Norway.

Finally, internet access showed the most pronounced disparity in the table, ranging from a mere 15% in Ethiopia to 98% in Norway — a gap that arguably reflects each country's level of overall development most starkly of all.

Quick Reference — 2-Minute Checklist Before You Write

Run through this before every timed practice
  1. ☐ Identified the highest and lowest values in each column
  2. ☐ Found the most striking gap (the biggest difference between any two cells)
  3. ☐ Grouped the data into 2–3 meaningful tiers or categories
  4. ☐ Identified any outliers or exceptions to the general pattern
  5. ☐ Chosen my overview pattern (overall winner, biggest gap, clear divide)
  6. ☐ Planned my two body paragraphs (each covering a different grouping)
  7. ☐ Selected 5–6 data points to include (NOT all of them!)
  8. ☐ Prepared at least one multiplier ("X times greater than")
  9. ☐ Ready to use exception language if there's an outlier
  10. ☐ Checked tense — past for dated tables, present for undated tables
