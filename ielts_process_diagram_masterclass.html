1️⃣ Introduction Templates: Hook Your Reader

The Paraphrase Formula

Process diagrams require you to paraphrase the given description and write an overview. Here's your winning structure:

The [diagram/chart/flowchart] illustrates/depicts/outlines [the process/the procedure/how X is produced/manufactured]. Overall, the process consists of/involves/comprises [number] [main stages/distinct steps/key phases], beginning with [first step] and culminating in [final step].
The diagram illustrates the process by which cocoa beans are transformed into chocolate. Overall, the procedure consists of ten distinct stages, beginning with harvesting and culminating in the production of chocolate products.
Why this works: Uses "illustrates" (stronger than "shows"), passive transformation verb ("are transformed into"), and overview with precise stage count. The phrase "culminating in" is sophisticated Band 8 vocabulary.
The flowchart depicts the various stages involved in brick manufacturing. From start to finish, the process involves seven key steps, commencing with the extraction of clay and ending with the delivery of finished bricks.
Why this works: "Depicts" adds variety, "commencing with" and "ending with" create a clear sequence frame. "From start to finish" is a natural collocation that signals comprehensive coverage.
The diagram outlines how plastic bottles are recycled. In general terms, the recycling process comprises nine successive phases, starting from collection and ultimately resulting in the creation of new plastic products.
Why this works: Perfect use of passive voice ("are recycled"), academic phrase "in general terms," and sophisticated ending "ultimately resulting in" shows advanced grammar (present participle clause).
The process diagram demonstrates the methodology employed in cement production. At a glance, it is evident that the procedure entails multiple interconnected stages, which span from the extraction of raw materials to the packaging of the finished product.
Why this works (Band 8+): "Methodology employed in" is academic vocabulary. "It is evident that" shows complex sentence structure. "Entails" and "interconnected stages" demonstrate sophisticated lexical resource. "Which span from...to" uses a relative clause expertly.
🚫 Common Mistakes to Avoid:
❌ DON'T use "shows" repeatedly → Use illustrates/depicts/outlines/demonstrates
❌ DON'T write "There are many steps" → Specify the exact number
❌ DON'T say "First...second...last" in the overview → Save detailed sequencing for body paragraphs
❌ DON'T forget the passive voice → Processes describe what happens TO things, not who does them

2️⃣ Key Description Vocabulary: The Passive Voice Powerhouse

Essential Passive Structures (Your Secret Weapon!)

Process diagrams LOVE passive voice because the focus is on what happens, not who does it.

[Material/Object] is/are [past participle] (by [agent - optional]).

Examples: collected, sorted, heated, crushed, mixed, filtered, dried, packaged, transported
In the initial stage, raw materials are gathered from various sources and subsequently transported to the processing facility.
Why this works: Two passive verbs in sequence ("are gathered," "transported") with sophisticated sequencing ("subsequently"). "Processing facility" is more academic than "factory."
Following this, the mixture is heated to approximately 1400 degrees Celsius in a rotating kiln, where it undergoes chemical transformation.
Why this works (Band 7+): Specific technical details (temperature, equipment name). "Undergoes chemical transformation" is sophisticated academic phrasing. The relative clause "where it undergoes" adds grammatical complexity.
The liquid is then passed through a series of filters to remove impurities, after which it is left to cool for several hours.
Why this works: Chain of passive constructions with clear purpose clause ("to remove impurities"). "After which" is an elegant connector. "Is left to cool" shows natural English phrasing.
Once the cooling process is complete, the solidified material is crushed into fine powder using industrial grinders and then is stored in large silos prior to packaging.
Why this works (Band 8): Complex time clause at the start, passive transformation "is crushed into," instrumental phrase "using industrial grinders," and advanced preposition "prior to packaging."
In the penultimate stage, the product is meticulously inspected for quality control purposes, ensuring that only items meeting the required standards proceed to the final phase.
Why this works (Band 8+): "Penultimate" is sophisticated vocabulary. Adverb "meticulously" enhances the verb. Present participle "ensuring that" creates a complex sentence with a clause showing result/purpose.
💡 Pro Tip: Vary Your Passive Verbs!
Don't repeat "is processed" five times. Use: is transformed, is converted, is refined, is treated, is subjected to, is modified, undergoes [transformation]. This lexical variety is what examiners look for in Band 7+ responses!
🚫 Common Mistakes to Avoid:
❌ DON'T use active voice unnecessarily → "Workers collect the materials" ✗ → "Materials are collected" ✓
❌ DON'T forget "by" phrases when needed → "is heated by machinery" (when agent is important)
❌ DON'T say "is process" → Use specific verbs: heated, cooled, mixed, filtered, etc.
❌ DON'T mix tenses → Processes are always in present simple passive

3️⃣ Comparison & Grouping Structures: Show Relationships

Parallel Process Descriptions

When processes have multiple pathways or simultaneous steps, use these comparison structures:

While one batch is being heated, another batch is simultaneously undergoing chemical treatment, thereby maximizing production efficiency.
Why this works (Band 8): "While" shows simultaneous actions, continuous passive "is being heated," sophisticated adverb "simultaneously," and academic phrase "thereby maximizing" with gerund.
At this point, the process diverges into two separate pathways: in the first route, materials are subjected to high-temperature treatment, whereas in the alternative route, they undergo chemical processing.
Why this works (Band 8+): "Diverges into" is academic vocabulary. Colon introduces parallel structure. "Whereas" creates sophisticated contrast. "Are subjected to" and "undergo" show varied passive constructions.
Unlike the previous stage which involved manual sorting, this phase relies entirely on automated machinery, which significantly accelerates the overall production timeline.
Why this works: "Unlike" creates effective comparison. Relative clause "which involved" adds complexity. "Relies entirely on" shows dependency. Second relative clause with "significantly accelerates" demonstrates advanced structure.
The initial stages share similarities in that both involve the collection and preliminary sorting of raw materials, however, they differ markedly in terms of the specific treatment methods employed.
Why this works (Band 8): "Share similarities" with "in that both" clause shows comparison. "Differ markedly in terms of" is academic phrasing. Perfect balance of similarity and contrast.
Grouping Template:
The process can be broadly divided into [number] main phases: [phase 1], [phase 2], and [phase 3]. The first phase encompasses [steps], while the second phase primarily focuses on [different aspect].
The entire procedure can be broadly categorized into three main phases: preparation, processing, and finalization. The preparation phase encompasses the collection, sorting, and cleaning of materials, whereas the processing phase primarily concentrates on thermal and chemical treatments.
Why this works (Band 7-8): "Can be broadly categorized" is academic. List structure with colon. "Encompasses" is sophisticated. "Primarily concentrates on" shows advanced vocabulary. Perfect parallel structure.
🚫 Common Mistakes to Avoid:
❌ DON'T overuse "but" → Use whereas, while, in contrast, conversely
❌ DON'T say "the same as" repeatedly → Use: similarly, likewise, in the same manner
❌ DON'T create unclear comparisons → Always specify what you're comparing
❌ DON'T force comparisons → Only compare when the diagram shows meaningful differences

Initially, the raw cocoa beans are harvested from cocoa trees and immediately transported to the processing plant. Following this, they are spread out on large trays to ferment for approximately one week.
Why this works: "Initially" starts sequence, "immediately" adds time precision, "following this" transitions naturally. Time specification "for approximately one week" adds detail.
Subsequently, the fermented beans are dried under direct sunlight, a process that typically takes five to seven days. Once the drying phase is complete, the beans are roasted at varying temperatures depending on the desired chocolate flavor profile.
Why this works (Band 8): "Subsequently" is sophisticated. Appositive phrase "a process that typically takes" adds detail. Time clause "once...is complete" shows complex structure. Dependent phrase shows variation.
At this juncture, the outer shells are removed, leaving the cocoa nibs, which are then ground into a thick paste known as cocoa liquor. Concurrently, sugar and other ingredients may be added to achieve the desired sweetness level.
Why this works (Band 8+): "At this juncture" is formal academic language. Present participle "leaving" shows result. Relative clause with passive "are then ground." "Concurrently" shows simultaneous action. "May be added" shows possibility appropriately.
In the penultimate stage, the chocolate mixture undergoes a conching process, during which it is continuously stirred and aerated for up to 72 hours. Ultimately, the refined chocolate is tempered, molded into various shapes, and finally packaged for distribution to retailers.
Why this works (Band 8+): "Penultimate stage" is sophisticated vocabulary. Relative time clause "during which" adds complexity. Specific time detail "up to 72 hours." Perfect chain of passive verbs with "ultimately" and "finally." Purpose phrase "for distribution to."
Prior to packaging, each batch is subjected to rigorous quality control testing to ensure consistency in texture, flavor, and appearance. Only after these tests are successfully completed does the product proceed to the final packaging stage.
Why this works (Band 9): "Prior to" is formal. "Is subjected to" is academic passive. Purpose infinitive "to ensure" with multiple nouns shows complex structure. Inverted structure "only after...does the product" is advanced grammar that impresses examiners.
⚡ Power Move: Time-Specific Details
Whenever the diagram shows time information, include it! "for 24 hours," "at temperatures exceeding 1000°C," "within a three-day period." These specific details boost your score by showing you can integrate information accurately.
🚫 Common Mistakes to Avoid:
❌ DON'T overuse "then" → Vary with subsequently, following this, after that, next
❌ DON'T use "firstly, secondly, thirdly" → Too mechanical; use more natural transitions
❌ DON'T say "after that it" → Use "following this, the material" or "subsequently, it"
❌ DON'T forget time prepositions → "for 2 hours" (duration), "in 2 hours" (deadline), "during this stage" (within period)

5️⃣ Data & Detail Integration: Place Information Smoothly

Where to Put Specific Details

Process diagrams often include technical details like temperatures, quantities, or time periods. Here's how to integrate them naturally:

The mixture is heated to approximately 1400 degrees Celsius in a rotating kiln, where it remains for roughly two hours.
Detail placement: Temperature (to + number), location (in + place), duration (for + time). "Approximately" and "roughly" show appropriate hedging for estimates.
At this stage, using high-pressure machinery, the material is compressed at a ratio of 10:1, reducing its volume significantly.
Why this works (Band 8): Equipment detail placed with "using," ratio integrated with "at a ratio of," result shown with present participle "reducing." Perfect integration of multiple details.
Through a series of filters containing increasingly fine mesh screens, impurities are systematically removed, with particles larger than 0.5 millimeters being trapped and discarded.
Why this works (Band 8+): Detail in prepositional phrase at start, "systematically" enhances verb, specific measurement integrated, "with + noun + being + past participle" structure shows advanced grammar.
The product, now cooled to room temperature (approximately 20-25°C), is transferred to storage containers, each with a capacity of 500 liters, where it can be kept for up to six months without deterioration.
Why this works (Band 9): Appositive phrase interrupts main clause elegantly, parenthetical temperature range, relative clause with capacity detail, purpose clause with time specification. Multiple complex structures showing mastery.
In a process that typically requires between four and six hours, the raw ingredients are gradually blended together in precise proportions (60% base material, 30% additives, and 10% binding agents) to create a homogeneous mixture.
Why this works (Band 8+): Relative clause with time range at start, adverb "gradually" modifies verb, "in precise proportions" setup, parenthetical percentages breakdown, purpose infinitive "to create" with technical term "homogeneous."
Technical Detail Integration Patterns:
• Temperature: heated to [X]°C | at temperatures ranging from [X] to [Y]
• Time: for approximately [X] hours | over a [X]-day period | within [X] minutes
• Quantity: containing [X] liters | comprising [X]% of the total | at a rate of [X] per hour
• Equipment: using [machinery name] | by means of [equipment] | through [device name]
• Ratio/Proportion: at a ratio of [X:Y] | in proportions of [X]% to [Y]%
🎯 Examiner Secret: Accuracy Over Approximation
If the diagram shows "1400°C," write "1400 degrees Celsius" not "a very high temperature." Examiners want to see you can accurately report specific data. But if it's approximate, hedge appropriately with "approximately," "roughly," "around."
🚫 Common Mistakes to Avoid:
❌ DON'T add invented details → Only report what's in the diagram
❌ DON'T say "very hot" → Use specific temperature if given, or "heated to high temperatures"
❌ DON'T overload one sentence → Spread details across multiple sentences for clarity
❌ DON'T ignore units → Always include: °C, kg, hours, meters, etc.

Band 6: Safe & Solid

First, the materials are collected. Then they are cleaned and sorted. After that, they are transported to the factory. Band 6
What works: Correct passive voice, clear sequence, simple connectors.
To improve: Combine sentences, use more sophisticated transitions, add detail.

Band 7: Competent & Clear

Initially, raw materials are collected and subsequently undergo cleaning and sorting processes. Following this, they are transported to the processing facility where further treatment takes place. Band 7
What works: Varied connectors ("initially," "subsequently," "following this"), complex sentence with relative clause, good range of passive structures.
To improve: Add more sophisticated vocabulary, integrate specific details, use advanced grammatical structures.

Band 8: Sophisticated & Precise

In the initial stage, raw materials are systematically gathered from various sources and immediately subjected to rigorous cleaning and sorting procedures, during which contaminants are meticulously removed. Once this preparatory phase is complete, the materials are transferred to the main processing facility, where they undergo a series of transformative treatments. Band 8
What works: Advanced vocabulary ("systematically gathered," "subjected to," "meticulously removed"), complex time clauses ("once...is complete," "during which"), sophisticated passive structures, natural integration of detail ("various sources," "rigorous," "transformative").
To reach Band 9: Add inverted structures, use more varied sentence openings, incorporate technical precision with hedging language.

Band 9: Masterful & Natural

At the outset of the procedure, raw materials from diverse geographical sources are systematically gathered and immediately conveyed to designated collection points, where they are meticulously assessed for quality prior to further processing. Only after passing these preliminary inspections do the materials proceed to the main facility, at which point they are subjected to an intensive regimen of cleaning procedures involving both mechanical and chemical treatment methods. This comprehensive approach ensures that contaminants, regardless of their nature or concentration, are effectively eliminated, thereby guaranteeing optimal purity levels for subsequent processing stages. Band 9
What makes this Band 9: Inverted structure ("only after...do"), sophisticated prepositional phrases ("at the outset," "at which point"), advanced vocabulary ("conveyed to," "designated," "intensive regimen"), complex noun phrases ("comprehensive approach," "optimal purity levels"), embedded clauses ("regardless of their nature"), sophisticated result expression ("thereby guaranteeing"), perfect integration of multiple grammatical structures seamlessly. Natural, fluent English that sounds like a technical report.
