Introduction Templates

Your introduction has two jobs: (1) Paraphrase the question and (2) Present an overview. This is your secret weapon—nail these two sentences and you're already scoring points!

Paraphrasing the Question Band 6+

[The line graph] illustrates / depicts / compares [the changes/trends in...] over [time period]
The line graph illustrates the changes in smartphone ownership rates among four age groups between 2010 and 2020.
Why This Works:
Changes "shows" → "illustrates" ✓ Paraphrases question vocabulary ✓ Identifies what the graph measures ✓ Specifies the time frame clearly
The graph compares the consumption of three different types of meat in a European country over a 25-year period from 1990 to 2015.
Why This Works:
Uses "compares" for multiple lines ✓ Specifies "three different types" (shows attention to detail) ✓ "25-year period from X to Y" is more sophisticated than just "between X and Y"
The provided line chart depicts fluctuations in international tourist arrivals to three Southeast Asian nations during the decade spanning 2005 to 2015.
Why This Works:
Band 8+ "Fluctuations" shows advanced vocabulary ✓ "decade spanning" is more elegant than "10 years between" ✓ Demonstrates range of expression

Don't Say...

❌ "The graph shows about smartphone ownership..." (too vague—specify WHAT about it)
❌ "This graph gives information..." (too generic and robotic)
❌ "From the graph we can see..." (never use "we" in Task 1)

Overview Statements Band 7+

Your overview is THE MOST IMPORTANT sentence in Task 1. It must identify the key features—usually the overall trends or most significant changes. Never include specific data here!

Overall, it is clear that [main trend 1], while [contrasting trend 2].

At first glance, it is evident that [most noticeable pattern], with [secondary observation].
Overall, smartphone ownership experienced an upward trend across all age groups, while the younger demographic showed the most dramatic increase.
Why This Works:
Identifies main trend (all groups increased) ✓ Highlights most significant feature (younger group) ✓ Uses "while" to show contrast ✓ NO specific numbers (perfect for overview!)
At first glance, it is evident that chicken consumption rose significantly throughout the period, whereas beef and lamb declined steadily.
Why This Works:
Band 8+ "At first glance, it is evident" is sophisticated ✓ Groups two similar trends (beef and lamb) ✓ Uses "whereas" for clear contrast ✓ Adverbs "significantly/steadily" add precision without data
Generally speaking, all three countries witnessed growth in tourist numbers, with Thailand maintaining its position as the most popular destination throughout the decade.
Why This Works:
"Generally speaking" = sophisticated opener ✓ "witnessed growth" = strong collocation ✓ "maintaining its position" shows stability (another trend type) ✓ Comparative superlative "most popular"

Pro Tip: The Overview Formula

Always ask: "If I could only tell someone TWO things about this graph, what would they be?" That's your overview! Look for: (1) Overall trends (rising/falling/stable), (2) Highest/lowest lines, (3) Lines that cross over, (4) Similar vs. different patterns

Trend Description Vocabulary

This is your bread and butter! Line graphs are all about describing HOW things change over time. Master these 30 power collocations and you'll never struggle for words again.

📊 Rising Trends (Increases)

experience a dramatic surge
Sales experienced a dramatic surge, rising from 200 to 500 units.
witness a sharp increase
The market witnessed a sharp increase in the final quarter.
climb steadily
Temperatures climbed steadily throughout the summer months.
grow exponentially
User numbers grew exponentially between 2015 and 2018.
rocket to [figure]
Prices rocketed to $150 per barrel by December.
soar dramatically
Exports soared dramatically, reaching an all-time high.
Between 2010 and 2015, the number of electric vehicles sold experienced a dramatic surge, climbing sharply from approximately 50,000 to over 500,000 units.
Why This Works:
Band 7+ Combines two rising verbs ("experienced a surge" + "climbing") ✓ Adverb "sharply" adds intensity ✓ "approximately" and "over" show data precision ✓ Time phrase at the start = clear organization

📉 Falling Trends (Decreases)

decline sharply/steadily
Coal consumption declined sharply after 2010.
plummet to [figure]
The figure plummeted to just 15% by 2020.
drop significantly
Attendance levels dropped significantly over the winter.
fall considerably
Prices fell considerably during the recession.
decrease marginally
The rate decreased marginally, by approximately 2%.
show a downward trajectory
Both indicators showed a downward trajectory.
From 2008 onwards, newspaper readership showed a downward trajectory, plummeting from 8 million to just 3 million readers by the end of the period.
Why This Works:
Band 8+ "showed a downward trajectory" = sophisticated phrasing ✓ "plummeting" emphasizes the dramatic fall ✓ "just 3 million" highlights how low it went ✓ Two time phrases (from + by) = excellent structure

➡️ Stability & Fluctuation

remain stable/constant
The rate remained relatively stable at around 30%.
level off at [figure]
Growth leveled off at approximately 500 thousand.
plateau at [figure]
Sales plateaued at 2 million units between 2015 and 2018.
fluctuate wildly/considerably
Oil prices fluctuated wildly throughout the decade.
remain unchanged
The figure remained virtually unchanged for five years.
hover around [figure]
Values hovered around the 50% mark for most of the period.
During the middle years, unemployment rates fluctuated considerably, hovering around 6% to 8% before eventually stabilizing in 2019.
Why This Works:
Shows complex movement (fluctuation + stabilization) ✓ "hovering around" with a range = precise description ✓ "before eventually" connects two phases beautifully ✓ Time markers organize the narrative

Don't Say...

❌ "The number went up" (too simple—use "rose/increased/climbed")
❌ "There was a big increase" (avoid "big/small"—use "dramatic/significant/modest/marginal")
❌ "It went down very quickly" (use "plummeted/dropped sharply/declined steeply")
❌ "The line went up and down" (use "fluctuated/showed volatility")

Comparison & Grouping Structures

Line graphs often show multiple lines—your job is to compare them intelligently! Group similar trends together and highlight contrasts. This shows analytical thinking (hello, Band 7+!).

Comparative Structures Band 7+

In contrast to Country A, where emissions rose steadily, Country B experienced a notable decline over the same period.
Why This Works:
"In contrast to X, where..." = sophisticated structure ✓ Shows opposite trends directly ✓ "over the same period" reinforces the comparison ✓ Relative clause ("where emissions rose") adds complexity
While the 18-25 age group saw smartphone ownership rise to 98%, those aged 65+ recorded a more modest increase to just 45%.
Why This Works:
Band 8+ "While X..., Y..." compares directly ✓ "more modest" and "just 45%" emphasize the difference ✓ Parallel structure (both reached X%) makes it elegant
France consistently outperformed Germany throughout the period, with the gap widening particularly after 2015.
Why This Works:
"outperformed" = advanced comparative verb ✓ "the gap widening" analyzes the relationship between lines ✓ "particularly after 2015" shows specific observation ✓ This is analytical, not just descriptive!

Power Comparatives & Superlatives

by far the highest/lowest
China had by far the highest production figures.
significantly/considerably higher than
Profits were significantly higher than the previous year.
marginally/slightly lower than
Sales were only marginally lower than projections.
nearly double/triple the figure
The 2020 figure was nearly double that of 2010.
rank first/last in
Japan ranked first in technology exports.
exceed [something] by [amount]
Revenue exceeded costs by $2 million.
By 2020, renewable energy consumption was nearly triple the figure recorded in 2005, making it by far the fastest-growing energy source.
Why This Works:
Band 8+ Mathematical comparison (triple) = precise ✓ "making it" links to a conclusion ✓ Superlative "fastest-growing" + "by far" = strong emphasis ✓ Two clauses connected smoothly

Grouping Similar Trends

Both beef and lamb consumption followed similar downward trends, falling by approximately 30% and 35% respectively over the 20-year span.
Why This Works:
Groups two similar lines efficiently ✓ "respectively" shows precision (30% for beef, 35% for lamb) ✓ "followed similar downward trends" = analytical phrasing ✓ Saves words while being clear
The three Asian markets—Thailand, Malaysia, and Singapore— demonstrated comparable growth patterns, all reaching peaks around 2018 before declining marginally.
Why This Works:
Dashes to insert examples = sophisticated punctuation ✓ "demonstrated comparable growth patterns" groups them analytically ✓ "all reaching peaks" shows they moved together ✓ Describes the shared pattern once (efficient!)

Comparison Secret Weapon!

When you see multiple lines, ask: "Which lines behave similarly?" Group those together in ONE sentence, then use separate sentences for lines that behave differently. This shows you can analyze, not just describe!

Don't Say...

❌ "Line A was bigger than Line B" (use "higher/greater/larger")
❌ "The numbers were different" (HOW different? quantify or qualify it!)
❌ Describing every single line separately without grouping similar ones
❌ "More higher than" (never combine "more" with comparative form)

Time & Sequence Expressions

Line graphs are time-based stories. Master these time expressions and prepositions to organize your response chronologically like a pro. This section alone can push you from Band 6 to Band 7!

Starting Points Band 6+

At the start of the period
At the start of the period, sales stood at 200 units.
In [year], / In the beginning,
In 2005, unemployment was recorded at 5%.
Initially, / At first,
Initially, both countries showed similar figures.
At the outset,
At the outset, renewable energy accounted for just 5%.
At the outset of the survey period in 2000, mobile phone subscriptions stood at a modest 15 million, representing only 20% of the population.
Why This Works:
"At the outset" = formal and sophisticated ✓ "stood at" = perfect verb for starting figures ✓ "a modest X" adds judgment ✓ Present participle "representing" adds extra detail smoothly

Middle Phases & Transitions Band 7+

Over the following [period]
Over the following decade, growth accelerated.
During the [time] period
During the 2010-2015 period, all sectors expanded.
From [year] to [year]
From 2008 to 2012, the trend remained stable.
Throughout the [period]
Throughout the entire decade, prices rose steadily.
In the interim years
In the interim years, fluctuations were observed.
Subsequently, / Thereafter,
Subsequently, the figure dropped to 30%.
Over the following five-year period from 2010 to 2015, the trend reversed dramatically, with exports declining from $5 billion to $3 billion.
Why This Works:
Band 8+ Combines "over the following [period]" with specific years = very precise ✓ "reversed dramatically" shows shift in direction ✓ "with + noun + -ing" = advanced structure for adding data
During the middle years of the study, between 2012 and 2016, both categories exhibited relative stability, hovering around the 40% mark.
Why This Works:
Two time phrases (general then specific) = excellent organization ✓ "exhibited relative stability" = sophisticated way to say "stayed similar" ✓ "hovering around" adds precision ✓ Great for describing plateaus

Endpoints & Conclusions Band 7+

By the end of the period
By the end of the period, the figure had tripled.
By [year], / In [final year],
By 2020, consumption had reached 500 million tons.
At the close of / Upon conclusion
At the close of the study, rates stood at 75%.
Ultimately, / Finally,
Ultimately, all three lines converged around 60%.
By the conclusion of the survey in 2020, electric vehicle sales had surged to 2.5 million units annually, representing a fifty-fold increase from the starting figure.
Why This Works:
Band 8+ "By the conclusion" = elegant formal expression ✓ Past perfect "had surged" = correct tense for completed action by a time point ✓ "fifty-fold increase" = mathematical precision ✓ Links back to starting point (full circle!)
Ultimately, by 2019, the gap between the two countries had narrowed considerably, with both nations recording approximately 80% internet penetration rates.
Why This Works:
"Ultimately" signals conclusion ✓ "had narrowed" (past perfect) = grammatically precise ✓ "with both nations recording" analyzes the convergence ✓ Shows relationship between lines at endpoint (analytical!)

Perfect Tense Mastery!

When you use "by [time]" for endpoints, use PAST PERFECT: "By 2020, sales had reached 5 million." When describing what was happening AT a specific time, use PAST SIMPLE: "In 2015, sales reached 3 million." Master this and examiners will love you!

Don't Say...

❌ "In 2010 to 2015..." (use "From 2010 to 2015" OR "Between 2010 and 2015")
❌ "During 2015..." (use "In 2015" for specific years)
❌ "At 2020..." (use "In 2020" or "By 2020")
❌ "After that, after that, after that..." (vary your transitions!)

Data Integration Techniques

This is where good responses become GREAT responses. Don't just list numbers—weave them smoothly into your sentences. These structures help you integrate data naturally while maintaining flow.

Embedding Data in Main Clauses Band 6+

Smartphone ownership among teenagers climbed sharply from 45% in 2010 to 92% in 2020, representing a gain of 47 percentage points.
Why This Works:
Data embedded with "from X to Y" = clear structure ✓ Present participle "representing" adds analysis ✓ Quantifies the change ("47 percentage points") = shows you can calculate ✓ Flows naturally
The year 2015 marked a turning point, with renewable energy accounting for 25% of total consumption, up from just 10% a decade earlier.
Why This Works:
Band 8+ "marked a turning point" = analytical observation ✓ "with + noun + -ing" = sophisticated structure ✓ "up from" efficiently compares two figures ✓ Tells a story with numbers

Using Relative Clauses Band 7+

China experienced the most substantial growth, reaching 500 million tons by 2018, which represented a threefold increase from the 2008 baseline.
Why This Works:
Relative clause "which represented" adds context ✓ Three pieces of data smoothly integrated (growth level, endpoint figure, comparison to start) ✓ "threefold increase from baseline" = sophisticated phrasing
The transportation sector, which accounted for 35% of emissions in 2000, saw its share increase to 42% by 2020, making it the largest contributor overall.
Why This Works:
Band 8+ Non-defining relative clause in middle = complex structure ✓ Compares start and end percentages ✓ "making it" draws a conclusion ✓ Multiple grammatical structures in one sentence!

Peak & Trough Language

reach a peak of [figure]
Sales reached a peak of 800,000 units in June.
hit a low of [figure]
The index hit a low of 1,200 points in March.
stand at [figure]
By 2019, the figure stood at exactly 5 million.
account for [percentage]
Coal accounted for 60% of energy production.
register [figure]
Unemployment registered 8.5% that quarter.
Oil prices reached a peak of $147 per barrel in mid-2008, before plummeting to just $40 by the end of that year amid the global financial crisis.
Why This Works:
Band 8+ Shows dramatic swing (peak to trough) ✓ "before plummeting" connects cause/effect ✓ "amid the global financial crisis" adds context (shows you understand WHY) ✓ Tells a complete story in one sentence

Approximation & Precision Language

approximately / roughly
The figure was approximately 2.5 million.
just under / just over
Sales reached just over 500,000 units.
around / about
Growth hovered around 3% annually.
exactly / precisely
The rate stood at precisely 15.7%.
nearly / almost
Consumption nearly doubled over the decade.
well over / well under
The total was well over 10 million by 2020.
Between 2012 and 2018, the figure fluctuated considerably, ranging from a low of approximately 2.8 million to a high of just under 4 million, with no clear directional trend.
Why This Works:
Uses approximation naturally ("approximately", "just under") ✓ "ranging from X to Y" = perfect for fluctuations ✓ Adds analytical comment "with no clear directional trend" ✓ Shows you can interpret data, not just report it

Variation is Everything!

Don't use "increase" five times! Rotate through: rise, grow, climb, surge, soar, expand. Same for data verbs: reach, hit, stand at, record, register. The examiner is counting—variety = higher score!

Don't Say...

❌ "The number is 500" (use "stands at / reaches / registers")
❌ "45.678%" (round to "approximately 46%" or "just under 46%")
❌ Only listing numbers without verbs: "2010: 20%, 2015: 35%, 2020: 50%"
❌ "About exactly 50%" (contradictory! choose one or the other)
